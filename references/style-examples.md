# Style Examples from Leading Reports

## Mandiant M-Trends Style
- Data-driven opening: "For the sixth consecutive year, exploits were the most frequently observed initial infection vector..."
- Specific metrics: median dwell time, percentage by vector, sector distribution
- Clear separation of observation vs. assessment
- Regional and sector breakouts

## CrowdStrike Global Threat Report Style
- Thematic title ("Year of the Enterprising Adversary", "Year of the Evasive Adversary")
- Named adversaries and activity clusters
- Breakout time metrics
- Strong focus on adversary tradecraft and speed
- Recommendations tied to observed behavior

## IBM X-Force Style
- Focus on initial access vectors and actions on objectives
- Industry targeting trends with percentages
- Credential abuse and identity as recurring themes
- Recommendations for fundamentals (patching, credential hygiene, access controls)

## Verizon DBIR Style
- VERIS framework language (Actors, Actions, Assets, Attributes)
- Incident Classification Patterns
- Clear distinction between incidents and breaches
- Industry and regional views
- "How to use this report" guidance for different readers

## Language Patterns to Emulate

**Good**:
- "Exploits accounted for 32% of initial access in investigations where the vector was identified."
- "We assess with moderate confidence that this activity is consistent with financially motivated actors."
- "Median dwell time was 11 days."

**Avoid**:
- "Hackers are running rampant..."
- "This unprecedented attack devastated the organization..."
- "Our cutting-edge platform detected..."
